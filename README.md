# Basketball Lineup & Rotation Decision Support Tool

## Project Overview

This project proposes a basketball analytics decision-support tool designed to help coaches and basketball operations staff make more informed lineup and player-rotation decisions. The tool would combine player performance, lineup statistics, opponent information, and game-context data to identify which player combinations are most effective in specific situations.

Rather than relying solely on traditional statistics such as points, rebounds, and assists, the proposed tool would provide a broader view of lineup effectiveness. It could highlight how different player combinations perform offensively and defensively, how performance changes against different opponents, and which lineups are most effective during specific game situations.

The goal is not to replace coaching judgment. Instead, the tool would provide additional evidence that coaches and basketball operations personnel could use when evaluating lineup and rotation decisions.

## Decision-Making Problem

Basketball coaches must make frequent decisions about starting lineups, substitutions, player rotations, and combinations on the court. These decisions can be difficult because individual player statistics do not always explain how players perform when playing together.

For example, a player may have strong individual scoring statistics but contribute less effectively when paired with certain teammates. Similarly, a lineup may have a lower individual scoring profile but perform particularly well because of defensive efficiency, rebounding, ball movement, or overall team performance.

The analytics problem is to identify which player combinations provide the greatest value in different situations and communicate that information in a way that can support coaching decisions.

## Proposed Analytics Approach

The proposed tool would use historical game and player-level data to evaluate lineup performance.

Potential data sources would include:

- Player points, rebounds, assists, steals, and blocks
- Field-goal and three-point percentages
- Turnovers and fouls
- Plus/minus statistics
- Offensive and defensive ratings
- Lineup combinations
- Minutes played together
- Opponent strength
- Home versus away games
- Game situation and score differential

The analysis could calculate lineup-level measures such as:

- Points scored per possession
- Points allowed per possession
- Net rating
- Effective field-goal percentage
- Turnover rate
- Rebounding percentage
- Performance by game situation

The results could then be displayed through an interactive dashboard showing the most effective lineup combinations and how their performance changes under different conditions.

No technical implementation is required for this proposal. The focus is on how the analytics idea could support organizational decision-making.

## Use by Decision Makers

A head coach could use the tool before or during games to evaluate potential lineup combinations and identify matchup-specific opportunities.

For example, a coach preparing to play an opponent with a strong interior offense could use the dashboard to identify lineups that have historically performed well defensively against larger or more physical teams.

A basketball operations executive could use the tool during roster planning to evaluate how players complement one another and identify potential weaknesses in the current roster.

The tool would present information through simple dashboards, charts, and lineup comparisons rather than requiring decision makers to interpret complex statistical models.

The final decision would remain with the coaching or basketball operations staff. Analytics would provide evidence to support the decision-making process.
